# רשת החיים – sources for content facts

## Amazon (`amazon`)

### Light and layers
- Emergent / canopy / understory / floor layers; little light reaches the floor (≈20% at Sea Acres, lower in dense tropical forest; the game says only "מעט מאוד אור"); saplings wait for gaps — https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/-/media/visitor/files/pdf/education/rainforests-of-nsw/rainforest-layers.pdf

### Plants
- **Kapok**: up to 60.4 m verified; huge buttress roots; night flowers mainly pollinated by bats; kapok fibre in pods; briefly deciduous — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceiba_pentandra
- Kapok in the Central Amazon pollinated mainly by *Phyllostomus* bats — https://core.ac.uk/works/18678888
- Harpy eagles nest on emergent trees such as *Ceiba pentandra*, also Brazil nut — https://oars.uos.ac.uk/1360/1/Harpy%20eagles.pdf
- **Brazil nut**: 30–50 m, emergent crown; fruit woody, up to 2 kg, 8–24 seeds; 14 months to mature; pollinated only by large, strong bees (incl. euglossine/orchid bees, mostly large females) that can lift the flower hood — https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Brazil_nut , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_nut
- Agoutis gnaw the pod, eat some seeds, cache others; forgotten caches germinate 12–18 months later; seeds may wait for a canopy gap — https://sweetgum.nybg.org/science/vh/narratives-details/?irn=18 , https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Brazil_nut , https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/20304/
- **Strangler fig**: seed deposited on a host branch by birds/bats; roots grow down and envelop the host; shades it and competes for water and nutrients — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangler_fig , https://www.nybg.org/blogs/science-talk/2014/01/an-unwanted-hug
- Fig leaves and fruits preferred by howler monkeys; fig fruits eaten by bats that disperse the seeds — https://floracostaricensis.myspecies.info/taxonomy/term/23222/descriptions
- **Cecropia**: fast pioneer of clearings (up to ~3 m/year); hollow stems house *Azteca* ants that get food bodies; fruit eaten by birds/monkeys — https://canopytower.com/cecropia
- *Azteca* ants reduce leafcutter attacks on *Cecropia* — https://repositorio.inpa.gov.br/handle/1/19336
- **Açaí palm**: clumping stems, flooded forests; fruit eaten by toucans, monkeys, tapirs, agoutis, fish, turtles; dispersed by birds, monkeys, water — https://fao.org/4/i2360e/i2360e04.pdf
- **Heliconia**: colourful parts are bracts; nectar-rich flowers pollinated by hummingbirds; fruits eaten by birds and mammals that disperse seeds — https://tambopatalodge.com/en/jungle-blog/heliconia-a-favorite-plant-of-the-amazons-hummingbirds
- **Tank bromeliads**: epiphytes on branches, not parasites; tanks hold water (sometimes litres); frogs and insects breed there; birds drink — https://www.tambopatalodge.com/en/jungle-blog/bromeliads-host-plants-for-amphibians-and-insects
- Hummingbirds are the main pollinators of most bromeliads — https://scielo.br/j/abb/a/tfbgY39Q7hys9jbVZHgYK7J/?lang=en
- **Epiphytic orchids**: aerial roots with velamen absorb water within seconds — https://asknature.org/strategy/aerial-roots-rapidly-absorb-water-and-nutrients/
- **Lianas**: climb trees to reach canopy light without a thick trunk; compete with trees; trees grow more/fruit more when lianas removed — https://earth.com/news/woody-vines-smothering-trees
- **Ferns**: shade-tolerant ground ferns dominate low-light floor — https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/-/media/visitor/files/pdf/education/rainforests-of-nsw/rainforest-layers.pdf
- **Glowing fungi**: luminescent *Mycena* in the Central Amazon on wood; green glow in Brazilian species; possible function (attracting insects that spread spores) is a hypothesis — https://ppbio.inpa.gov.br/en/node/1798 , https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/living-lights/ , https://roundglasssustain.com/wild-vault/bioluminescent-fungi
- **Giant waterlily**: leaves up to 3 m; spines underneath; flowers open at night white, pink on second night; beetle-pollinated — https://www.kew.org/plants/giant-waterlily ; spines possibly against herbivorous fish and manatees — https://stories.rbge.org.uk/?p=15311

### Animals
- **Leafcutter ants**: cut leaves, carry to nest, grow fungus that feeds larvae; colonies of millions (3.55 million example); Hebrew name נמלים גוזרות עלים; small workers ride on leaves as guards — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leafcutter_ant , https://www.lbscience.org/?p=13941
- **Orchid bees**: males collect orchid fragrances in modified hind legs; major orchid pollinators — https://www.life.illinois.edu/scameron/Euglossini/index.html
- **Blue morpho**: blue from light-reflecting scales; brown underside with eyespots; adults drink juice of rotting fruit and sap — https://www.rainforest-alliance.org/species/blue-butterfly , https://tropicalstudies.org/rbt/attachments/volumes/vol23-1/07-Young-Morpho.pdf
- **Hummingbird**: nectar plus insects/spiders for protein; drinks up to 2× body weight per day; torpor-like rest at night — https://a-z-animals.com/animals/hummingbird/what-do-hummingbirds-eat/
- **Poison dart frog**: eats ants and small insects; toxins related to diet; warning colours; diurnal; tadpoles ride on parent's back; carried to water in bromeliads — https://www.rainforest-alliance.org/species/poison-dart-frog/ , https://amphibiaweb.org/species/1641 , https://www.joelsartore.com/keyword/tadpole/
- **Toucan**: light spongy bill; mostly fruit; eats figs, *Cecropia* and palm fruits; key disperser of large-seeded palms — https://www.scielo.br/j/bjb/a/JdHp88gsbCjbGZZNbptmG4r/?lang=en , https://noticiasambientales.com/animals/what-is-their-most-important-role-five-facts-about-toucans/ , https://www.wgbh.org/news/2013-05-31/big-mouthed-toucans-key-to-forest-evolution
- **Macaw**: fruits, nuts, seeds, flowers, nectar; clay licks for minerals; nests in large tree cavities; palm fruit and figs favourites; strong beaks crack hard shells — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_macaw , https://a-z-animals.com/animals/macaw/macaw-facts/what-do-macaws-eat/
- Red-and-green macaw can open Brazil nut pods (only other animal besides agouti per source) — https://www.rainforestexpeditions.com/wildlife/agouti/
- **Sloth**: fur hosts algae, moths, beetles; descends ~once a week; leaf diet, a leaf can take up to 30 days to digest; brown-throated sloth feeds primarily on *Cecropia*; predators jaguar and harpy eagle — https://www.calacademy.org/explore-science/slothful-ecosystems-0 , https://slothconservation.org/what-do-sloths-eat-sloth-diet-food-and-digestion/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-throated_sloth
- **Agouti**: opens Brazil nut pods with teeth; scatter-hoards; forages by day; escapes into water — https://www.rainforestexpeditions.com/wildlife/agouti/
- **Howler monkey**: strictly plant food (leaves, flowers, fruits); calls heard >2 miles; prehensile tail; disperses seeds in droppings — https://www.rainforest-alliance.org/species/howler-monkey/ , https://askabiologist.asu.edu/plosable/seed-dispersal-primate-poop
- **Jaguar**: good swimmer, hunts in rivers; prey fish, turtles, caimans, deer, peccaries, capybaras, tapirs; ambush from trees; rosettes — https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/animals/mammals/jaguar/ , https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/jaguar/diet ; agouti in scats — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4803351/table/pone.0151814.t001 ; sloth predator — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-throated_sloth
- **Harpy eagle**: diet dominated by sloths in the central Amazon; howler monkeys also frequent prey — https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de26324c5aef494fda63ed , https://oars.uos.ac.uk/1360/1/Harpy%20eagles.pdf
- **Capybara**: largest rodent; partly webbed feet; strong swimmer; stays underwater up to 5 min; groups; grasses and aquatic plants; jaguar main predator — https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/things-you-might-not-know-about-capybaras.html , https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/capybaras/diet , https://www.tambopatalodge.com/en/jungle-blog/capybara-the-worlds-largest-rodent-roams-tambopatas-amazon-forests
- **Lowland tapir**: mainly fruit plus leaves/shoots; seeds pass intact → disperser; nocturnal/crepuscular; eats *Cecropia* and palm fruits — https://colombia.wcs.org/en-us/STRATEGIC-PILLARS/SPECIES/LOWLAND-TAPIR.aspx , https://museucienciesjournals.cat/en/abc/issue/46-1-2023-abc/scientific-and-traditional-knowledge-meet-diet-of-the-lowland-tapir-tapirus-terrestris-in-the-orinoquia-region-of-colombia
- **Fruit bat (*Artibeus*)**: figs and many tropical fruits; carries fruit away and disperses seeds; bats disperse *Cecropia* seeds into open areas — https://www.batcon.org/bat/artibeus-jamaicensis/ , https://tropicalstudies.org/rbt/attachments/volumes/vol48-1/14_Garcia_Seed_dispersal.pdf
- **Giant anteater**: no teeth; sticky tongue ~60 cm, flicked up to 150×/min; up to 30,000 insects a day; feeds ~1 min per mound then moves on; walks on its fists with claws curled (keeps them sharp); bushy tail used as a blanket; pup rides on mother's back ~1 year; active day or night depending on area — https://seaworld.org/animals/facts/mammals/giant-anteater/ , https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/node/174 ; leaf-cutting ants (*Atta*, *Acromyrmex*) found in its diet (→ diet `leafcutter`) — https://www.redalyc.org/journal/457/45760865005/
- Jaguar preys on giant anteaters and coatis (kills + scats table; jaguars significantly prefer giant anteater) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4803351/table/pone.0151814.t001 , https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2015.00148/text
- **Kinkajou**: nocturnal, sleeps in tree hollows by day; hangs by its prehensile tail; ~90% ripe fruit, figs the largest part; drinks nectar, pollen collects on its face; pollinates balsa (*Ochroma*); fig seeds pass intact and are dispersed in droppings (→ relation `fig` disperses); harpy eagle is a predator (→ harpy diet) — https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/kinkajou/diet-feeding , https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/kinkajou/behavior-ecology
- **South American coati**: diurnal; omnivore (fruit, invertebrates, larvae, spiders); long flexible snout searches under leaf litter and rotten logs; females and young in bands of up to ~30, males solitary; ankles rotate so it climbs down head-first; sleeps in trees; ringed tail held upright on the ground (may help the band keep track of each other). No documented link to a specific plant in this game was found, so no diet/relation targets — https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Nasua_nasua/ , https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/node/544 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_coati

## Israeli Mediterranean woodland (`grove`)

### Plants
- **Palestine oak (אלון מצוי)**: evergreen; wind-pollinated; main bloom April; acorns wait ~a year on the tree then ripen in late autumn; eaten by rodents and corvids — https://www.kalanit.org.il/botanical_news/quercus-calliprinos-09-2020/?print=pdf
- Oaks survive fire: roots resprout (Carmel fire); *Q. calliprinos* regrows fast after cutting — https://israel-tourguide.info/blog/aftermath-carmel-fire , https://ltsermontado.pt/?q=node/11
- **Palestine terebinth**: deciduous; bright red leaves when shedding; blooms March–April, wind-pollinated; fruit red → purple-blue; seeds dispersed by birds; aphid galls — https://wildflowers.co.il/english/plant.asp?ID=228 , https://www.tiuli.com/flora/10/%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%94-%D7%90%D7%A8%D7%A5-%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA
- **Carob**: evergreen; blooms in autumn (Aug–Jan); wind + insect pollination; pods take a year, ripen through early summer; eaten by animals; good honeybee forage in the dearth period — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carob , https://wildflowers.co.il/english/plant.asp?id=236 , https://reformjudaism.org/blog/galilee-diary-carobs , https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/an-evaluation-of-israeli-forestry-trees-and-shrubs-as-potential-f/ , https://aday.ciu.edu.tr/en/news/ciu-cbprc-has-detected-honey-which-has-botanical-origin-carob-first-time-northern-cyprus
- **Aleppo/Jerusalem pine**: serotinous cones opened by fire release seeds; pines regenerate from seed after fire; pine understory shrubs feed gazelles — https://jgpausas.blogs.uv.es/2020/11/ , https://israel-tourguide.info/blog/aftermath-carmel-fire , https://ynetnews.com/environment/article/rJid0zM4P
- **Mastic**: evergreen shrub; wind-pollinated (blooms Feb–May); fruit green → red → black, ripens autumn; eaten and dispersed by birds incl. blackbird — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistacia_lentiscus , https://www.mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/183.html , https://wildflowers.co.il/english/plant.asp?id=268 , https://jecologyblog.com/2024/02/28/elena-quintero-disentangles-the-lasting-effects-frugivore-birds-have-in-the-early-recruitment-of-plant-species/
- **Palestine buckthorn** (*Rhamnus lycioides* subsp. *graeca* = *R. palaestina*): spiny shrub, deciduous subspecies; flowers late Mar–May; birds eat fruit, shelter and nest in it, disperse seeds; highest density of bird-dropped seeds under *Rhamnus* in Israeli scrub; a major gazelle food — https://jbg.gardenexplorer.org/taxon-41573.aspx , https://www.cretanflora.com/Rhamnus%20lycioides%20oleoides%20text.html , https://www.mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/290.html , https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/seed-shadows-generated-by-frugivorous-birds-in-an-eastern-mediter/ , https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/26732/
- **Greek sage (מרווה משולשת)**: abundant in Israel; oily leaves with lavender-like chemicals; flowers late Mar–June; honeybees harvest its pollen — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvia_fruticosa , https://cretanflora.com/salvia_fruticosa_text.html
- **Rockrose**: blooms March–June; each flower lasts one day; pollen-rich, little nectar; honeybees and other bees visit; fire triggers mass seed germination (heat shock) — https://www.wildflowers.co.il/english/plant.asp?ID=15 , https://mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/8204.html , https://www.usgs.gov/publications/role-charred-wood-heat-shock-and-light-germination-postfire-phrygana-species-eastern
- **Cyclamen (רקפת מצויה)**: flowers October–April; partial shade, rock crevices; fruit stalk lengthens downward and plants the capsule in soil/crevices; summer dormancy as a tuber; seeds dispersed by ants that eat the sticky coating — https://www.kalanit.org.il/botanical_news/cyclamen-persicum/?print=pdf , https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Cyclamen , https://www.cyclamen.org/field-studies/cyclamen-society-field-study-israel-1990/
- **Crown anemone (כלנית מצויה)**: blooms through winter, peak Jan–Feb (Shvat); opens mornings, closes toward evening and on rainy/cold days; leaves appear after first rains; pollinated by bees and beetles; honeybees main visitors in the first weeks; seeds with fluffy hairs dispersed by wind; tuber — https://www.kalanit.org.il/botanical_news/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%93%D7%A9-%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%98-%D7%9B%D7%9C%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99%D7%94/?print=pdf , https://wildflowers.co.il/english/plant.asp?ID=272 , https://ratio.huji.ac.il/node/3129128
- **Mushrooms**: appear after first autumn rain (or early spring); decompose dead matter; mycorrhizal partners of oaks and pines (minerals ↔ sugars) — https://mountainsmagleb.com/2020/10/07/the-ecological-niche-of-mushrooms-voyage-into-the-marvelous-world-of-mushrooms-in-the-woods/

### Animals
- **Honeybee**: main visitors of early-blooming anemones; visit rockrose and sage; collect carob nectar — https://ratio.huji.ac.il/node/3129128 , https://mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/8204.html , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvia_fruticosa
- **Butterfly** (generic): butterflies occasionally visit rockrose for nectar — https://mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/8204.html
- **Harvester ants (*Messor*)**: in Israeli Mediterranean woodland; collect seeds and store them in granaries; seed predators that also redistribute seeds (change seed rain) — https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.07.16.549226.full.pdf , https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Harvester_ant , https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/harvester-ants-modify-seed-rain-using-nest-vegetation-and-granivo/
- **Fireflies**: 8 species in Israel; adults active late March to September; light signals for males and females to meet; disturbed by light pollution — https://smnh.tau.ac.il/en/when-did-you-last-see-a-firefly/ ; larvae eat snails and slugs — https://hort.extension.wisc.edu/articles/fireflies/
- **Eurasian jay**: type locality of subsp. *atricapillus* is Jerusalem; caches acorns in autumn; one bird can spread >1000 acorns/year; credited with spreading oaks; eats insects, seeds, fruits; mimic — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_jay , https://woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/animals/birds/jay ; acorns cached singly, 3–550 m from source in a Mediterranean landscape — https://www.uv.es/jgpausas/papers/PonsPausas2007-Oecol_acorn-dispersal.pdf
- **Blackbird**: frugivore + insects, snails, earthworms; disperses seeds; eats *Rhamnus* and *Pistacia lentiscus* fruit — https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/turdus_merula , https://www.eurekalert.org/multimedia/764178 , https://jecologyblog.com/2024/02/28/elena-quintero-disentangles-the-lasting-effects-frugivore-birds-have-in-the-early-recruitment-of-plant-species/
- **Chameleon**: Israel in range; trees/shrubs; diurnal; tongue 1.5–2× body length; independent eyes that converge when hunting; colour changes with light/temperature/mood; eats flies, hymenopterans (bees), crickets, grasshoppers, beetles — https://www.lbscience.org/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/276 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_chameleon , https://www.mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/179.html
- **Starred agama (חרדון מצוי)**: rocky areas, basks on rocks/walls; dives into cracks; mainly insects especially ants, bees, wasps; also snails and plants; lighter when warm, darker when cold — https://www.marwell.org.uk/?p=5046 , https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Stellion , https://initiative-pim.org/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lo-Cascio-P.-et-al_2001_Nisyros_Seasonal-variations-of-the-diet-of-Laudakia-stellio-Agamidae-from-Nisyros-island-Dodecanese.pdf
- **Spiny mouse (קוצן מצוי)**: Israel; rocky scrub; nocturnal; mostly seeds, also snails; sheds tail to escape — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_spiny_mouse , https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/diet-selection-and-energy-and-water-budgets-of-the-common-spiny-m/ ; barn owls hunt spiny mice in Israel — https://core.ac.uk/works/1444118
- **Porcupine**: nocturnal; eats roots, bulbs, tubers, fruits; largest rodent in the region; rattles hollow tail quills as warning; digging pits collect water, organic matter and seeds, used as germination sites — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_crested_porcupine , https://mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/489.html , https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/observations-on-the-feeding-habits-of-the-indian-crested-porcupin/ ; *Cyclamen* storage organs and acorns staples of crested porcupine diet (Italy, sister species) — https://usiena-air.unisi.it/retrieve/e0feeaa7-ebed-44d2-e053-6605fe0a8db0/Feeding%20habits%20of%20the%20crested%20porcupine%20Hystrix%20cristata.pdf
- **Hedgehog (*Erinaceus concolor*)**: in Israel; nocturnal, hides by day in bushes/rocks/leaf piles; insects up to grasshopper size, ~20% of body weight per night; hibernates in cold months; also slugs, snails, worms; ants (esp. *Messor*) dominate Mediterranean hedgehog diet — https://mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/185.html , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_white-breasted_hedgehog , https://www.ensv.dz/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/djennoune2018.pdf
- **Rock hyrax**: colonies among rocks; morning basking; sentinel with alarm calls; herbs/annuals in winter–spring, tree leaves in summer–autumn; moist rubbery soles act as suction cups; mostly herbivorous — https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/rock-hyrax , https://www.tiuli.com/fauna/37/%D7%A9%D7%A4%D7%9F-%D7%A1%D7%9C%D7%A2 , https://www.mahmiyat.ps/en/flora-and-fauna/677.html , https://www.israel-in-photos.com/rock-hyrax.html
- **Mountain gazelle**: up to 80 km/h; grass and leaves; diet changes with season; in Israeli shrubland dominated by *Rhamnus*, also *Ceratonia*, *Pistacia*; *Quercus* in autumn — https://jerusalemzoo.org.il/animals-en/mountain-gazelle , https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/26732/ , https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Gazella_gazella/
- **Red fox**: rodents, birds, insects, carrion, fruit; hunts by hearing, pounces; mainly nocturnal — https://www.pbs.org/wnet/nature/animal-guide-red-fox/2212 , https://agris.fao.org/search/ar/records/65deac0c0f3e94b9e5d05a75 ; spiny mice main mammal prey of foxes in Israeli study (Blanford's fox) — https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/diet-and-foraging-behavior-of-blandfords-foxes-vulpes-cana-in-isr/
- **Barn owl**: hunts at night; rodents 95% of diet in Israel; a pair + chicks eat 2,000–6,000 rodents/year; uses nest boxes (doesn't build nests); heart-shaped facial disc focuses sound; hunts in total darkness — https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frym.2023.1182137/pdf , https://www.barnowltrust.org.uk/owl-facts-for-kids/barn-owl-hearing/

- **Hoopoe (דוכיפת)**: chosen national bird of Israel (May 2008); mostly insects, larvae dug out / probed from the ground with the full length of the bill; ants among common prey; forages on the ground; nests in holes in trees, cliffs or walls; nestling/female secretion smelling of rotting meat deters predators — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_hoopoe ; crest raised when excited; pinkish-brown body, black-and-white wings, down-curved bill — https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/hoopoe ; insects, larvae, worms; very short tongue, tosses prey into the air and swallows it — https://www.tiuli.com/fauna/60/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%AA
- **Spur-thighed tortoise (צב יבשה מצוי, *Testudo graeca*)**: *T. g. terrestris* occurs in Israel; verified lifespans over 100 years — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spur-thighed_tortoise , https://lafeber.com/vet/basic-information-sheet-greek-or-spur-thighed-tortoise/ ; in Israel peak activity Jan–Apr, long summer–autumn activity hiatus, found mostly in shrub margins (stable micro-climate) where they rest and sleep; study population does not hibernate — https://www.ramat-hanadiv.org.il/en/mehkarim/circle-of-life-of-the-species-testudo-graeca-in-israel/ , https://sdg.haifa.ac.il/?p=12288 ; wild diet mostly leaves, flowers eaten with the leaves, fruit sporadically — https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/ah/ah-1648.pdf ; hatchlings find food on their own — https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3813286,00.html

## חושבים כמו חוקרים (1.3, the lab's five small experiments: src/lab/thinking.js)

The habits themselves (school science, not facts about creatures):
- **Fair test:** change only one thing and keep the rest the same, so the result can be trusted — https://www.educationquizzes.com/ks1/science/investigating-whose-plant-will-grow-best/ .
- **Observation and inference:** an observation is what is actually seen; an inference is how we explain it, and it can change with new evidence (the tracks scene follows the "Tricky tracks" lesson) — https://edu.rsc.org/resources/tricky-tracks-observation-and-inference-in-science-11-14-years/4017168.article .
- **Repeating a measurement:** several readings are combined to reduce random error (the game takes the middle one, the median, which one odd reading cannot spoil) — https://eurachem.org/index.php/publications/leaflets/pubsilsqrtn .
- **Correlation and causation:** two things can rise together because a third thing drives both; only a change that we make (an experiment) shows the cause — https://www.innerdrive.co.uk/blog/correlation-and-causation/ .

What the experiments show:
- **Seedlings in the dark** grow long stems, pale yellow or white, with small leaves (etiolation) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etiolation , https://bio.libretexts.org/Courses/Norco_College/BIO_5%3A_General_Botany_(Friedrich_Finnern)/09%3A_Environmental_Responses/9.04%3A_Etiolation_and_Shade_Avoidance .
- **Too much water:** roots need oxygen as well as water; waterlogged soil leaves them without air — https://swyg.nmsu.edu/2018/052618.html .
- **Sandy soil** holds little water and few nutrients — https://www.purdue.edu/hla/sites/yardandgarden/what-is-loam/ .
- (The heights in the greenhouse are a game model, not measured numbers. Two pots given the same come out a little different on purpose: natural variation.)
- **An egg sinks in fresh water and floats in salt water** — https://www.sfu.ca/physics/about-us/outreach/activities/activity/understanding-buoyancy.html .
- **Dead Sea water** is about 1.24 kg per litre (salinity about 34%), so things float easily — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea .
- **Apples float:** about a quarter of an apple's volume is air — https://vidyaenews.most.gov.lk/science-insider/have-you-ever-tried-putting-an-apple-in-water/ .
- **An orange floats with its peel and sinks peeled:** the peel is full of air pockets — https://melscience.com/GB-en/articles/does-orange-float-or-sink/ . (That a peeled orange and an egg float in Dead Sea water follows from the density above.)
- **Pumice floats** (full of gas bubbles) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumice .
- **Ice floats:** it is less dense than water (about 0.917) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice .
- **Gazelle tracks:** two toes (hooves) make an upside-down heart shape, like deer tracks — https://www.wildernesscollege.com/identify-deer-tracks.html ; the mountain gazelle is a bovid (even-toed ungulates) and cannot go very long without water — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_gazelle .
- **Snails** leave a silvery slime trail and eat irregular holes in leaves (caterpillars make similar holes); look for them after dark with a flashlight — https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/snails-and-slugs/ ; land snails are most active at night and in damp weather — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snail .
- **Drops on a cold glass** come from water vapour in the air that condenses on the cold surface — https://fdacourse.iri.columbia.edu/documents/M1U2_Clouds/Clouds_Readings/S3_Clouds_Readings/S3_Condensation_The_Water_Cycle_USGS.pdf , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensation .
- **Mushrooms come up after rain** — https://news.uga.edu/?p=25515 ; **snails become active after rain** — https://pir.sa.gov.au/news/pestfacts/recent-rain-has-snails-and-slugs-moving-what-it-means-for-your-crop .
- **More flowers bring more bees:** floral abundance raises insect visits — https://sro.sussex.ac.uk/id/eprint/63437/ .

## The lab's stations (1.3: the aquarium, the greenhouse and the cabinet as small worlds; src/lab/aquarium.js, greenhouse.js, cabinet.js, texts in src/lab/station-text.js)

The aquarium, "שונית בקופסה": the rules of the week follow the reef data of the game (src/data/reef.js and the reef section above): the clownfish lives in the anemone, the pistol shrimp digs and the goby keeps watch (also https://utmsi.utexas.edu/science-and-the-sea/radio-program/cooperation/ : the shrimp "digs a burrow", the goby "acts as a lookout" and wiggles to warn it), the moray and the octopus hide in holes, the lionfish rests under table coral and eats goby and shrimp (lionfish stomachs "mostly fin fish" — https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/charlotteco/2017/02/16/lionfish ), the moray hunts the octopus.
- **Small fish shelter in branching coral** from predators (damselfish) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5958076 (title and topic seen in search results).
- **Grazers keep algae off corals:** herbivorous fish graze algae that would otherwise "outcompete corals for space and sunlight, eventually smothering them" — https://coral.org/blog/guardians-of-the-reef-the-importance-of-herbivore-fish-in-coral-reef-health/ ; parrotfish and urchins graze algae (Parrotfish line above; reef.js).
- **Butterflyfish eat coral polyps** ("long mouth ... like forceps to pluck off individual coral polyps") — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corallivore .
- **Cleaners need clients;** cleaner stations work by day (Bluestreak cleaner wrasse line above).
- (The eight places, "a patch of algae feeds two grazers", "a branching coral hides three small fish" are game rules.)

The aquarium, "יום ולילה":
- **Parrotfish sleep in a mucus cocoon** every night; the leading idea (a hypothesis) is protection from small night parasites (gnathiid isopods), when cleaner fish are not active — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/parrotfish-sleep-in-a-mosquito-net-made-of-mucus .
- **Coral polyps stretch out their tentacles at night** to catch plankton — https://www.livescience.com/63190-coral-polyps-feeding.html .
- **Morays hunt at night, by smell** (Moray line above); **the urchin hides by day and scrapes algae at night** (reef.js).
- **Bleaching:** when the water is too warm the polyps expel their algae and turn pale or white; they can recover, "but not if it stays too hot for too long" — https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/coral-bleaching ; a rise of 1–2 °C above the usual can trigger bleaching, strong light with calm clear skies adds to it, and corals that survive can regain their algae if conditions improve quickly — https://floridadep.gov/sites/default/files/3-SEAFAN-Bleach-Fact-Sheet.pdf .
- **Eilat's corals tolerate heat better than most** (Fine, Gildor & Genin 2013, line above) — also https://archive.israel21c.org/eilats-corals-resistant-to-effects-of-global-warming/ .
- (Three heat steps, "two weeks of heat and sun turn it white", and the recovery speed are game values.)

The greenhouse, "מגדלים חמנייה":
- **Sunflowers need sun,** at least 6–8 hours a day — https://ucanr.edu/node/168463 ; in shade a seedling stretches, pale and weak (Etiolation line above); too little and too much water (lines above).
- **Snails eat soft seedlings at night** (Snails lines above).
- **Aphids suck plant sap; ladybirds eat aphids,** each one hundreds — https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/wakullaco/2016/05/19/aphids-are-the-insect-worlds-villain , https://pender.ces.ncsu.edu/2022/05/ladybugs-on-the-prowl (it also says aphids do little lasting harm: in the game a plant left with aphids only ends a little weaker).
- **Bees give a sunflower more seeds:** seed yield was 43% higher in plants visited by pollinators (Scientia Agricola 2011) — https://revistas.usp.br/sa/en/article/view/22722 .

The greenhouse, "זרעים יוצאים לדרך":
- **Seeds spread away from the parent plant** so the young plant has room, water and light of its own — https://nj.gov/dep/parksandforests/natural/heritage/docs/bl_Seed_Dispersal_Strategies_4.4.24.pdf .
- **Wind, animals, water:** maple-like winged seeds spin, fluffy (dandelion-like) seeds fly long distances, burdock's tiny hooks stick to fur and inspired Velcro, fruit-eating animals carry seeds away, a coconut floats on its air pocket — https://bbg.org/news/learn_how_seeds_travel_and_design_your_own_seed_activity ; terebinth seeds are spread by birds (grove section above).
- **Aleppo (Jerusalem) pine seeds have a wing (about 20 mm) and are wind-dispersed** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_halepensis .
- **The squirting cucumber (ירוקת החמור)** shoots its seeds out by itself, some about 10 m, at over 20 m/s — https://physicsworld.com/a/the-mechanics-of-squirting-cucumbers-revealed/ (7–8 m: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecballium ); native around the Mediterranean, including the "Palestine" botanical region that covers Israel — https://www.kew.org/plants/squirting-cucumber . The game says "a few metres".
- (All distances are game values; the goal line is 8 m.)

The cabinet, "ממיינים כמו חוקרים":

**Rainforest (amazon)**
- Light and layers: the floor gets only about 2% of the sunlight; the densest biodiversity is in the canopy; understory plants have much larger leaves; rapid decay, nutrient-poor soil (ae24, ae25, ah28) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest
- Drip tips let leaves shed rain quickly and dry, so fewer epiphytes and fungi grow on them (ah25) — https://northernwoodlands.org/outside_story/article/drip-tips-and-lance-shaped-leaves
- The Amazon forest: over half of the remaining rainforest; ~390 billion trees of ~16,000 species; half of the region's rain is produced by the forest; Saharan dust fertilises it; ~9 feet of rain a year; one in five bird species (ae26, ah26, ah27, ah69, ah70) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rainforest
- The Amazon river: largest discharge, 20% of river water reaching the oceans; no bridge across its full width; rises more than 9 m and floods the forest; the dark Rio Negro and the sandy Amazon run side by side without mixing (ae27, ae28, ae29, ah71) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River
- Kapok: briefly deciduous and flowers while leafless; large thorns on trunk and branches; buttresses 12–15 m up the trunk (ah29–ah31) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceiba_pentandra
- Brazil nut: fruits almost only in pristine forest, plantations yield little; lives 500 years or more, up to a thousand; agoutis and macaws among the few that open the fruit (ah32–ah34) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_nut , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agouti
- Strangler fig: the host may die, leaving a column with a hollow core (ah35) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangler_fig
- Cecropia: Müllerian food bodies for its *Azteca* ants, which keep off herbivores and vines; leaves and buds the sloths' main food; umbrella crown; seeds pass undigested and are spread by animals (ae69, ah36, ah37) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecropia
- Bromeliads: the pineapple is a bromeliad; tanks hold up to 10 L with insect larvae, spiders and small frogs (ae30, ah38) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromeliaceae
- Orchids: vanilla comes from an orchid; dust-like seeds need a fungal partner; many deceive their pollinators (ae31, ah39, ah40) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchidaceae
- Lianas: up to 600 m long; bridges in the canopy; they tie weak trees to strong ones, and a falling tree can pull others down (ae34, ae35, ah41) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liana
- Ferns: no flowers or seeds, spores under the fronds; young fronds unroll from a fiddlehead; tree ferns up to 20 m (ae32, ae33, ae70) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern
- Glowing fungi experiment in Brazil: fake mushrooms lit with green LEDs caught more insects than dark ones, and the insects spread the spores (ah42) — https://geiselmed.dartmouth.edu/news/2015/researchers-discover-why-some-mushrooms-glow/
- Giant waterlily: a 2 m leaf grows in seven days; air trapped between the ribs keeps it afloat; the flower traps the beetle and turns pink (ae36, ah44, ah45) — https://www.kew.org/plants/giant-waterlily ; one leaf bore 226 kg; the flower closes at nightfall with the beetles inside; ribs like girders (ah43, ah45) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_amazonica
- Leafcutter ants: carry up to 50 times their weight; castes by size (the smallest tend the fungus); waste carriers and a refuse heap; they stop collecting a leaf that harms the fungus; mounds over 30 m across (ae37, ah46–ah49) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leafcutter_ant
- Orchid bees: brilliant metallic green, gold and blue (ae38) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euglossini
- Morpho: structural colour, not pigment, studied for fabrics, dye-free paints and anti-counterfeit marks; brown underside with eyespots; drinks the juice of fermenting fruit and tree sap (ae39, ah50, ah51) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morpho_(genus)
- Hummingbirds: up to 99 wingbeats a second in small species, hovering; heart up to 1,260 beats a minute; torpor at night; the bee hummingbird is the smallest bird (ae40, ae41, ah52, ah53) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hummingbird
- Poison dart frogs: captive-bred frogs have no significant toxins (diet); warning colours; some under 1.5 cm; in some species the mother feeds the tadpoles eggs (ae42–ae44, ah54) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poison_dart_frog
- Toucan: nests in tree hollows; bill under the wing in sleep may insulate it; the bill is the largest relative to body of all birds, 30–50% of the body surface; it regulates heat (ae45, ae46, ah74) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toco_toucan ; infrared/thermal cameras showed it (ah55) — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/oversized-beak-keeps-toucan-cool-15023806/
- Scarlet macaw: typical lifespan 40–50 years; calls carry many kilometres (ae47, ah66) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_macaw
- Sloths: strong swimmers (13.5 m a minute against ~4 m a minute in the trees); come down about once a week (every eight days); long curved claws hang without effort (ae48–ae50) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloth
- Howler monkeys: enlarged hyoid bone; males call at dawn (ae51, ah56) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howler_monkey
- Jaguar: rosettes with a spot in the middle; black jaguars documented; biggest cat in the Americas; caimans, turtles and fish near water (ae52–ae54, ah57) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar

- Kinkajou: 13 cm tongue for nectar; "honey bear", a relative of coatis and raccoons; rotates ankles and feet 180° (ae64, ae65, ah65) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinkajou

**Reef**
- Coral reefs: "rainforests of the sea"; less than 0.1% of the ocean area but at least 25% of marine species; grow faster in clear sunny water; 1–3 cm a year sideways; a fringing (shore) reef is attached to the shore; the Red Sea's fringing reefs among the best developed (re24, re25, re31, rh26, rh27) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef
- Corals: fragments start new colonies; in bright shallow water the algae can supply most or all of the daily energy; mass spawning at night after a full moon (re30, rh28, rh29) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral (Eilat spawning: Shlesinger & Loya 2019, above)
- *Acropora* (table coral): fast-growing, a finger-sized fragment becomes a ball-sized colony in 1–2 years; especially susceptible to bleaching (rh30, rh31) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acropora
- Fire coral: long, hair-like stinging polyps come out of the pores; mostly yellow or orange, also brown, green, blue (re69, rh32) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millepora
- Pulsing soft corals in Eilat: pulsing sweeps water away and photosynthesis is about ten times higher; a light touch by a diver stops the pulsing for a few minutes (rh33) — https://www.technion.ac.il/en/blog/2013/04/a-200-years-old-mystery-solved-scientists-from-the-technion-and-the-hebrew-university-at-the-inter-university-institute-in-eilat-discovered-why-soft-corals-pulsate/
- Red Sea: one of the saltiest seas (high evaporation, little rain, no significant rivers); more than 1,200 fish species, 10% endemic; about 200 kinds of soft and hard coral (re26, re27, re29) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea
- Gulf of Aqaba (Eilat): up to 1,850 m deep; 24 km at its widest, 160 km long (re28, rh63) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba
